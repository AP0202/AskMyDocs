SECTION: What This Medication Is For
Metformin is used to help control blood sugar levels in people with type 2
diabetes. It works by reducing the amount of sugar released by the liver and
helping your body respond better to insulin.

SECTION: How to Take It
Take 500mg by mouth twice daily with meals (once with breakfast, once with
dinner) unless your provider tells you otherwise. Taking it with food reduces
stomach upset. Do not crush or chew extended-release tablets.

SECTION: Common Side Effects
Nausea, diarrhea, stomach upset, and a metallic taste are common, especially
in the first few weeks. These often improve over time. Taking the medication
with food can help.

SECTION: Serious Side Effects - Seek Care If You Notice
Unusual muscle pain, difficulty breathing, unusual tiredness, stomach pain
with nausea and vomiting, or feeling cold, especially in the arms and legs.
These can rarely be signs of a serious condition called lactic acidosis and
require prompt medical attention.

SECTION: Monitoring
Your provider will periodically check your kidney function and blood sugar
control (A1C) while you are on this medication, typically every 3-6 months.

SECTION: Interactions and Precautions
Tell your provider about all medications and supplements you take. Let your
provider know before any procedure involving contrast dye (used in some
imaging studies), as metformin may need to be paused around that time.
Limit alcohol intake, as it can increase the risk of side effects.

SECTION: Plain-Language Summary
Metformin helps manage type 2 diabetes by lowering blood sugar. Take it with
food to reduce stomach side effects, which are common but usually improve.
Watch for the rare but serious warning signs listed above. Keep up with
regular lab monitoring as recommended by your provider. This sheet is for
general information and does not replace instructions from your prescriber.
