SECTION: Test Summary
A lipid panel measures the fats (lipids) in your blood, including cholesterol
and triglycerides. It is commonly used to assess cardiovascular risk.

SECTION: Total Cholesterol
Result: 218 mg/dL
Reference Range: less than 200 mg/dL
Flag: HIGH
Plain-language note: Total cholesterol combines several types of cholesterol.
A result above 200 mg/dL is generally considered borderline-high to high and
may warrant lifestyle changes or further evaluation.

SECTION: LDL Cholesterol ("bad" cholesterol)
Result: 142 mg/dL
Reference Range: less than 100 mg/dL (optimal)
Flag: HIGH
Plain-language note: LDL cholesterol can build up in artery walls over time.
Higher LDL is associated with increased cardiovascular risk. Diet, exercise,
and sometimes medication can help lower LDL.

SECTION: HDL Cholesterol ("good" cholesterol)
Result: 52 mg/dL
Reference Range: greater than 40 mg/dL (men), greater than 50 mg/dL (women)
Flag: NORMAL
Plain-language note: HDL helps remove other forms of cholesterol from the
bloodstream. Higher HDL is generally considered protective.

SECTION: Triglycerides
Result: 168 mg/dL
Reference Range: less than 150 mg/dL
Flag: HIGH
Plain-language note: Triglycerides are a type of fat used for energy. Elevated
levels can be related to diet, weight, alcohol intake, or underlying metabolic
conditions.

SECTION: Interpretation Notes from Lab
This panel shows elevated total cholesterol, elevated LDL, and mildly elevated
triglycerides, with normal HDL. This pattern is often addressed with dietary
changes, physical activity, and follow-up testing. This is not a diagnosis of
any cardiovascular condition.

SECTION: Next Steps
1. Discuss these results with your provider to determine your overall
   cardiovascular risk, which depends on more than this panel alone.
2. Ask about lifestyle changes such as diet and exercise.
3. Ask whether a follow-up lipid panel or additional testing is recommended.
